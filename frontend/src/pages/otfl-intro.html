ON THE FAREWELLED LAND

Intro

By Xavier Wang

Chua pressed half his face past the rough bark, the rest of his body still fused to the tree as if it might swallow him whole. Beyond him stretched a field of reeds—a tall, whispering reed bed. It was late autumn. The stalks had turned a weary yellow-brown, brittle with age, yet their tips still held onto streaks of green, stubborn and alive.

The world lay unnaturally still.

That stillness made the cicadas sound deafening, their shrill cries cutting through the silence, abrupt and intrusive. Chua knew one of them was somewhere out there, hidden among the reeds. Waiting. Or maybe it was just him who was waiting.

His stomach twisted sharply. Twenty hours—he'd counted, in seconds. Twenty hours since he'd last eaten. The hunger had dulled into something heavier now, a slow, gnawing emptiness. It didn't matter. He wouldn't eat anytime soon. Not while they were still out there.

And they weren't leaving.

A sudden crack tore across the field.

Chua flinched but didn't dare look. His body locked in place, breath shallow, as if even the air might betray him. Thoughts spiraled wildly—fragmented, uncontrollable. The first that surfaced was him.

That man.

The one who had taken him in when the famine hollowed out his village. The one who taught him how to survive, how to hide, how not to die.

His chest tightened. His heart pounded so hard it hurt.

What happened to him?

The question struck like a blow, and Chua's vision wavered. A dark blot crept in from the edges, swallowing the world piece by piece until everything blurred into black.
